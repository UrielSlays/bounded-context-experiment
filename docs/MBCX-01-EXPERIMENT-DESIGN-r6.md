# MBCX-01 Executable Bounded Obligation Continuity Experiment Design r6

## 0. Status and deciding question

This is an executable preregistration design, not a harness implementation or authorization for paid inference.

The question is whether one logical conductor can preserve exact due-obligation continuity using a bounded decision working set while dormant archive size and active-subject depth grow, which of FD, FC, or TN offers the defensible engineering carrier, and whether one frozen model can use the selected packet in a single cold A0 -> B -> writer -> A1 episode.

Three conclusions remain separate:

1. continuity: at least one candidate is exact and bounded on H and D at fixed frontier;
2. mechanism engineering: the FD/FC/TN Pareto set under physical accounting;
3. conditional model usability: BC is noninferior to oracle-minimal OM while OM clears the frozen floor.

TN may lose without killing continuity. The model may fail without killing mechanical continuity. No production, cross-model, concurrent-writer, security, personality, procedural-memory, or coordination-stage claim is made.

## 2. Build-decision challenge and controls

MBCX-01 is not evidence that a special brain object should exist. It is a kill-shot against the narrower hypothesis that exact selective continuity can remain bounded by the active decision frontier rather than total dormant history.

Controls are full or rolling context where feasible; exact ordinary files; structured reconstruction; BM25; sparse+dense RAG; namespaces or blackboard state; oracle-minimal and oracle-all-relevant packets; and deliberately unbounded raw-replay FD. If a simpler control matches exactness, reconstruction, isolation, and physical cost, the special mechanism earns nothing.

## 3. Canonical world and mechanisms

The canonical scientific world is an append-only journal plus immutable payloads. Generator, reference reducer, gold, packet reference, and candidate producers are separate.

- FD: indexed derivation from journal/payload state.
- FC: derived materialization including segmented snapshot plus bounded tail.
- TN: typed derived relations, conflicts, and provenance.

All arms emit one DecisionPacketV2. A fourth comparator must demonstrate a public semantic object unavailable to all three; branding and storage strategy are insufficient.

## 4. State, writer, and packet

The v2 state/action contract freezes bitemporal cuts, restoration, immutable scope, obligation and dependency lifecycle, seventeen event types, safe canonical numbers, catalogs, action enumeration, writer validation, and execution.

ACT:{scope}:{obligation} addresses an obligation; it is not the completion_action_id. The writer reads the latter from canonical state, constructs complete_obligation, validates the ordered action set, and atomically appends children plus one commit terminal. Invalid B output always appends one reject_handoff_result with a frozen code.

Every accepted A0, B, and A1 response is appended through record_call_response before parsing. Recovery reuses the exact bytes without resampling.

The packet contract closes DecisionPacketV2, TaskPacketV2, and WorkerHandoffV2. No packet admits free-text reasoning, transcript, scratch, arbitrary config, or unbounded arrays.

BC and OM share outer structure, descriptions, instructions, action order, output contract, byte envelope, padding, token maximum, and transport settings. OM contains minimal public semantic objects; BC contains candidate-selected objects. ID permutation and a deterministic unpaid discriminator test length, token count, position, and padding leakage. Byte-identical served inputs share one stochastic call.

## 5. Frozen workloads

Stage 1 has 240 independent scenarios: six motifs times forty seeds, with 24 deterministic cuts. Motifs are correction/restoration, obligation lifecycle, dependency reversal, cross-scope collision, delegation/idempotent handoff, and mixed delayed A/B/A continuity.

For Stage 2, generate 80 candidates per motif before model work. An eligible hash-selected cut has exactly one mounted critical due obligation, at least one non-gold action, and schema-valid BC/OM packets. Sort eligible candidates by SHA-256 of scenario_id, cut_id, and stage2-cut-v2; retain the first 64 per motif. Hash every eligibility decision and final 384-ID manifest. A shortfall is DESIGN_OR_IMPLEMENTATION_FAIL.

Analysis is SHA-256 scenario order with looks at 96, 192, and 384.

Dormant archive values H are 128, 1024, 10000, and 100000. Active-depth values D are 1, 4, 16, 64, and 256. Frontier values F are 1, 4, 16, 64, and 256.

Exactness crosses every H at D=16 and 256; every D at H=1024 and 100000; the F curve at H=100000/D=16; and the corner H=100000/D=256/F=16. H and D twins preserve final state, packet semantics, action universe, gold, and observation.

F=16/64/256 are deterministic resource cells outside the paid action envelope. Required overflow returns FRONTIER_LIMIT before model dispatch.

## 6. Mechanical and boundedness gates

Across Stage 1, axes, crosses, mandatory traces, and red candidates require zero lost due obligations, wrong-scope gold/admission, stale resurrection, duplicate effect, reducer/action/scorer/packet/reconstruction disagreement, taint, unregistered read, hidden fallback, or undeclared overflow.

The measurement contract owns the closed counter set, unit classes, boundaries, per-counter bounds, unit-total bounds, preprocessing, caches, background work, jitter, and crosses. Counts, bytes, pages, operations, and branches are not normalized by one common slope. Every counter and same-unit total must stay within its absolute bound. The HD-interaction and counter-split red candidates must fail. A new counter fails the run.

The engineering result is the Pareto set over exactness, registered physical work, maintenance, cold p95, artifact bytes, rebuild time, and branches. Carrier selection is separately labeled and has no engineering authority.

If no candidate passes exactness and H/D bounds, bounded continuity is falsified for the workload. If one passes, it survives regardless of TN.

## 7. Isolation and reconstruction

Candidate children receive only public journal/payloads, the five contracts, candidate implementation, governed dependency API, empty artifact/output roots, and closed config. They cannot read generator, reducer, reference packets, gold, scenario secrets, another candidate, source tree, ambient directory, or network.

A fresh sanitized child must fail on withheld/corrupt/nonce-only artifacts; survive source-directory rename; swap decoded state with swapped artifacts; change only causal output after a field mutation; and rebuild projection and packet identically from journal. Every PID is captured, bounded, awaited, terminated if needed, and verified absent. All work is charged.

## 8. Stage-2 DAG

Each scenario runs BC and OM in frozen balanced order. Each condition has three accepted calls:

1. A0 receives DecisionPacketV2 and emits the unique target plus TaskPacketV2.
2. B receives only TaskPacketV2 and emits WorkerHandoffV2.
3. A1 runs after response journaling, deterministic writer action, teardown, and cold reconstruction and emits the post-handoff action set.

At n=384 the maximum is 2304 accepted calls and 768 reconstructions before pre-acceptance retry overhead. One retry is allowed only when no response was accepted and route policy classifies the failure pre-acceptance.

## 9. Inference and missingness

The inference contract targets average conditional means over the heterogeneous frozen manifest. Primary intervals use bounded-martingale Hoeffding bounds with Bonferroni protection across two streams and three fixed looks. No common-mean or unlimited optional-stopping claim is made.

The noninferiority margin is -0.10 and the OM floor is 0.80. OM remains inferential. The owner sees branch reachability, expected/max calls, the inconclusive region, and feasible operating characteristics before paid authorization.

OC cells are explicit nonnegative paired probabilities summing to one. No impossible cell is filtered at runtime. Missing pairs jointly enumerate every BC/OM completion through seven missing pairs; any branch variation is TRANSPORT_INCONCLUSIVE. Eight missing pairs is terminal.

Corrected upstream confseq blobs are historical comparator pins only. The r7 method is independently implemented from its formula and fixtures.

## 10. Transport and authorization

A no-paid route soak measures accepted/no-body, route identity, fallback, cache, transform, and parameter rejection and publishes the per-call reliability requirement for the six-node pair.

The preauthorization manifest names endpoint, provider, model snapshot, tokenizer, route, fallback/cache/transform policy, maximum and expected calls, retry semantics, token maxima, prices, time, reconstructions, dollar ceiling, and every input hash.

No paid call occurs without separate owner authorization naming endpoint and maximum spend.

## 11. No-model gates

Gate 0: five committed pinned artifacts and scoped claims.

Gate 1: independent reducer/enumerator, shared-entity pair fixtures, fourteen red witnesses, exact schemas/renderer, and frozen Stage-1/Stage-2 manifests.

Gate 2: FD/FC/TN parity, render discriminator at chance, closed config, taint canaries, and unique-input dedupe.

Gate 3: all counter and total gates, crossed H/D witness, F overflow, reconstruction, and child lifecycle.

Gate 4: exact V2 packets, unique A0 gold, node response journal, deterministic rejection/commit, exactly-once effect, and cold A1 equality.

Gate 5: formula parity, feasible OC manifest, all decision/missing branches, route soak, explicit stopping/power/cost, and separate paid authorization.

Failure blocks the next stage. No unregistered mid-run fix is allowed.

## 12. Decisions and non-claims

Primary decisions are DESIGN_OR_IMPLEMENTATION_FAIL; BOUNDED_CONTINUITY_FALSIFIED; BOUNDED_CONTINUITY_SURVIVES with Pareto set; and the conditional inference branch: promote, model/interface fail, model/interface ceiling, transport inconclusive, or inconclusive/underfunded.

No result is a persistent mind, latent transfer, universal memory, production readiness, or coordination-stage success.
