1. Top-level verdict

NO-GO(undercontrolled strong-flat comparator + architecture-sensitive workload frame + statistically disproportionate full-support gate)

r3 is substantially stronger than the experiment shape implied by its own change log: it fixes the destructive-flat-baseline problem, holds presentation much more tightly, removes outcome-driven replacement, makes reconstruction materially causal, removes the invalid chi-square pilot authority, and makes transport failure conservative.

I still would not authorize the 12-pair pilot from this design.

Three P1 classes remain capable of changing the architecture decision:

FM is not yet the strongest cheap flat competitor. r3 permits flat replay/current projection but forbids FM from transiently deriving several decision-relevant continuity facts that a cheap event-sourced projection can derive without persisting typed objects. That can manufacture a TN win.

The confirmatory sampling frame is insufficiently architecture-neutral. Exact top-level mechanism quotas do not freeze the difficulty, nesting, ambiguity, overlap, or restoration structure within those mechanism classes. A 9:3 A mix therefore leaves enough generator discretion to move a +0.03 threshold.

The literal “full feasible” Student-t operating-characteristic requirement is disproportionate. A legal maximum-variance two-point delta family forces the first passing n to 8,700 paired scenarios under the r3 rule. That implies roughly 1.05–1.40 million model calls before unspecified oracle/control fractions and transport loss. At the same time, a genuinely exhaustive two-support search over the exact delta lattice is enormous; a hand-selected finite family grid can miss the very legal distribution the design says it is protecting against.

There is a fourth P1 process/specification problem: several answer-changing objects—transition semantics, generator grammar, exact packet schemas/instruction bytes, target tokenizer, identifier grammar, and canonical joint-maximum fixtures—are intentionally frozen after this council. The design therefore cannot yet be implemented without scientific choices that this seat was unable to inspect.

The correct next step is r4 design recut, not pilot.

E4. “Complete” support problem

There are 470,395 exact attainable delta values.

The number of unordered distinct two-support pairs is:

110,635,492,815

Including singleton supports:

110,635,963,210

Therefore the design has a dilemma:

“exact finite two-point cases” means all legal pairs ⇒ the brute-force manifest is absurdly large; or

it means a selected family of pairs ⇒ the gate is not a proof over the full feasible distribution space and can omit a worse legal family.

That is exactly the Attack-E question the packet asked.

E5. Clopper-Pearson direction

For r3's acceptance semantics:

power ≥ threshold → use a lower one-sided CP bound;

coverage ≥ threshold → use a lower one-sided CP bound;

false-win ≤ threshold → use an upper one-sided CP bound;

false-kill ≤ threshold → use an upper one-sided CP bound.

Exact-binomial guidance treats upper and lower one-sided limits as distinct objects; r3 does not presently state the orientation.

It also does not specify the simulation-error accounting when many families and candidate n values are searched.

E6. Alternative direction

Bounded-mean literature gives nonparametric, finite-sample, variance-adaptive confidence intervals/sequences for observations bounded in a known interval; that is directly matched to delta ∈ [-1,1].

I am not declaring one of those methods validated for MBCX-01 from citations alone.

I would instead freeze one published bounded-mean algorithm, impose a hard Nmax/spend ceiling, and run the same no-model operating-characteristic attack against it before adoption.

Missing-pair tipping arithmetic

If m of N target pairs are missing and each can be any delta in [-1,1], the possible mean interval widens by:

2m / N

Examples:

N=48,  m=1  -> width 0.04167
N=96,  m=1  -> width 0.02083
N=192, m=1  -> width 0.01042
N=384, m=1  -> width 0.00521

Thus at small n, one ambiguous pair can already erase a +0.03 conclusion. That is conservative, but it is exactly what prevents informative censoring from choosing the winner.

10. WOULD YOU DESIGN IT DIFFERENTLY?

Yes.

The conceptual difference is small but important.

r3 asks approximately:

Can a typed continuity package beat a flat package that remembers the events and does ordinary traversal but is not allowed to expose typed continuity interpretations?

I would ask:

Can typed-native persistent structure beat the strongest cheap event-sourced alternative that is allowed to recompute every decision-relevant public fact from those same events at read time?

That is the architecture decision I would actually want before accepting typed complexity.

I would also move the final sealed design review after the no-model manifest is frozen, not before it. A council cannot certify architectural neutrality of prompt schemas, grammar, tokenizer pressure and transition semantics it has never seen.

Finally, I would treat affordability as a hard experimental constraint, not merely an outcome of an unlimited n search. A disposable Stage-A instrument should be allowed to conclude:

“This effect cannot be distinguished economically enough to justify the architecture.”

That itself is useful product information.
