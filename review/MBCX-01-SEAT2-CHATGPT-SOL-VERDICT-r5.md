10. WOULD YOU DESIGN IT DIFFERENTLY?

At the architecture level: no. At the preregistration boundary: yes.

I would make three changes.

First, move every answer-making rule out of prose and into frozen machine-readable specifications. The design document should explain; it should not be the only place the state machine exists.

Second, separate deterministic stress density from paid inference density. Use all 24 cuts in Stage 1, where they are free and useful. For Stage 2, preselect exactly one obligation-bearing handoff cut per independent scenario. The deciding question is whether the model can use the packet through a cold handoff—not whether it can survive 24 serial paid handoffs before the scenario earns a 1. This reduces the literal paid workload by 24× without weakening Stage-1 semantic coverage.

Third, make every materializer read go through one instrumented dependency surface. The useful rule to borrow from Bazel/Skyframe is essentially: if a dependency can affect the output, the engine must register/read it through the governed dependency graph; hidden reads invalidate incremental correctness.
