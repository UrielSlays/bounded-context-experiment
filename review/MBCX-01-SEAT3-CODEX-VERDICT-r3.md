## 6. Required attacks A–H

### Attack A — treatment/materializer parity

**Input world**

Current time `12:00`; active scope `alpha`.

- `s1`: `alpha.power = degraded`, source `e10`.
- `s2`: `beta.bridge = open`, source `e11`.
- `o1`: alpha action `a1`, due `11:59`, prerequisites `[s1, o2]`.
- `o2`: explicitly cross-scope beta obligation, action `a2`, prerequisite `[s2]`, not independently due/near-due.
- `o5`: alpha action `a5`, near-due `12:04`.
- `o3`: alpha obligation due `11:58`, completed/tombstoned.
- `o4`: open gamma obligation due `12:01`, neither active-scope nor explicitly cross-scope.

**Method**

I implemented the r2 selector rules as deterministic in-memory reducers and materialized compact logical packets.

**Outputs**

R2-style F and TF flat packet:

```json
{
  "scope": "alpha",
  "now": "12:00",
  "states": [
    {"id":"s1","key":"power","value":"degraded","source":"e10"}
  ],
  "obligations": [
    {"id":"o1","action":"a1","tier":"due","prereq_ids":["s1","o2"]},
    {"id":"o5","action":"a5","tier":"near_due","prereq_ids":[]}
  ],
  "terminal_ids":["o3"]
}
```

TN packet:

```json
{
  "scope":"alpha",
  "now":"12:00",
  "assertions":[
    {"id":"s1","scope":"alpha","key":"power","value":"degraded","current":true,"evidence":["e10"]},
    {"id":"s2","scope":"beta","key":"bridge","value":"open","current":true,"evidence":["e11"]}
  ],
  "obligations":[
    {"id":"o1","action":"a1","status":"open","tier":"due","requires":["s1","o2"]},
    {"id":"o2","action":"a2","status":"open","requires":["s2"]},
    {"id":"o5","action":"a5","status":"open","tier":"near_due","requires":[]}
  ],
  "edges":[["o1","o2"],["o2","s2"]],
  "terminal":[{"id":"o3","status":"completed"}]
}
```

`o4` is correctly excluded from every packet, and `o3` cannot be re-executed.

**False TN win**

TN exposes `o1 → o2 → s2`; F exposes only `o2` as an ID. A breadth-first search over F’s own obligation/state index, capped at r2’s depth `3`, width `3`, and closure `12`, would recover the same actionable closure cheaply. R2 nevertheless reserves “typed multi-hop closure” for TN.

**False TN loss**

Using identical actionable semantics, a compact F/TF logical packet occupied `90` structural proxy units while the TN packet occupied `180` because of type tags, edge objects, provenance, and currentness fields. Under a proxy cap of `160`, F succeeds and TN returns `budget_failure`, even though the additional fields do not change the action decision.

**Interpretation**

- The shared materializer catches ordinary current-scope, due/near-due, terminal, and direct-reference mistakes.
- It does **not** catch the false TN win because r2 labels bounded traversal “typed-native” even though F has the necessary IDs.
- Density logs expose the false TN loss’s size symptom, but do not decide whether the encoding was an efficient implementation of the treatment.
- Gate 1 catches it only if this exact joint case is declared non-overflow. That declaration is presently unfrozen.

**Limitations**

These are exact logical packets, not exact model-token packets. R2 has not selected the schema or tokenizer needed for a token-exact comparison.

### Attack B — retraction and public-schema neutrality

**Input trace 1: correction and retraction**

1. `e1`: assert `color = blue`.
2. `e2`: correction targeting `e1`, new value `red`.
3. `e3`: retraction targeting `e2`.

**Trace**

| Step | F | TF/TN |
|---|---|---|
| `e1` | `blue` | `blue` |
| `e2` | `red` | `red`, with `blue` retained as superseded history |
| `e3` | `unknown` | `blue` restored |

**Input trace 2: cancellation and retraction**

1. `e4`: open obligation `o7`.
2. `e5`: cancellation targeting `e4`.
3. `e6`: retraction targeting `e5`.

| Step | F | TF/TN |
|---|---|---|
| `e4` | `o7` open | `o7` open |
| `e5` | removed/tombstoned | canceled, prior open state retained |
| `e6` | cannot restore; `unknown`/absent | `o7` restored open |

**Interpretation**

TN succeeds here because it retained legitimate public history, not because it received private gold. However, typed ontology is not necessary for that retention: a flat append-only journal of the same primitive events can replay the effective state. The comparison therefore tests “current-only overwrite versus history retention” unless `F+` is added.

Public event types such as correction, cancellation, and retraction are not inherently unfair. The remaining neutrality risk is that generator composition and nested transition semantics are written in the same ontology as TN rather than against an independent transition algebra.

**Limitation**

This trace validates deterministic semantics only; it does not prove the synthetic workload’s frequency or composition is architecture-neutral.

### Attack E — full decision-rule calibration

**Method**

I implemented double-precision regularized beta/gamma functions, Student-t CDF/quantiles, chi-square inversion, and exact binomial enumeration for two-point distributions. For a rare-low family with probability `0.05` at `-1`, the remaining support point was chosen to produce the required mean.

**Threshold-bug check**

The win rule now correctly evaluates whether the lower 95% endpoint exceeds `+0.03`. `+0.08` is used as a power alternative, not an observed-mean gate. The r1 threshold bug is gone.

**Rare-low false-win and coverage witness at true mean `+0.03`**

The distribution had:

- Probability `0.05` at `-1`.
- Probability `0.95` at `0.0842105263`.
- SD approximately `0.2363`.

| n | False-win probability | Student-t coverage |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 48 | `0.08526` | `0.91229` |
| 60 | `0.04607` | `0.95108` |
| 96 | `0.13581` | `0.86114` |
| 384 | `0.05124` | `0.93733` |

The nonmonotonicity is caused by lattice/binomial support crossing the interval boundary as `n` changes.

**Complete four-condition witness**

For paired rare-low/rare-high two-point families, searching `n` in increments of 12 found the first candidate satisfying all endpoint conditions plus coverage at:

`n = 516`

| Operating characteristic | Result | Required |
|---|---:|---:|
| False win at mean `+0.03` | `0.04065` | `≤ 0.05` |
| Win power at mean `+0.08` | `0.98070` | `≥ 0.80` |
| Kill power at mean `0` | `0.82981` | `≥ 0.80` |
| False kill at mean `+0.08` | `0` | `≤ 0.05` |
| Coverage | `0.94707` | `≥ 0.94` |

This does not mean `n=516` is the experiment’s final requirement; it demonstrates why the retained distributions and parameter grids must be frozen and evaluated under the complete rule.

**Pilot variance bound**

For `m=12`:

- `chi2_ppf(0.05, 11) = 4.5748130793`.
- Multiplier `sqrt(11 / 4.5748130793) = 1.5506352969`.

| Pilot SD `s` | `s_upper` | `s_design` |
|---:|---:|---:|
| `0` | `0` | `0.20` |
| `0.10` | `0.1550635` | `0.20` |
| `0.20` | `0.3101271` | `0.3101271` |

Rare-tail miss probabilities in a 12-pair pilot:

- Tail probability `0.05`: `0.95^12 = 0.54036`.
- Tail probability `0.10`: `0.90^12 = 0.28243`.

Thus the SD floor and mandatory rare-tail families are load-bearing.

**Interpretation**

I do not recommend replacing Student-t as the primary interval. A transparent frozen interval combined with full-rule operating-characteristic calibration is defensible. Merely switching to bootstrap-t or BCa is not an automatic improvement for discrete/lattice data; recent work also documents bootstrap-t pathologies and BCa undercoverage in some discrete settings ([bootstrap study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10083)).

The repair is to:

- Freeze distribution-support grids and retention rules.
- Enumerate finite-support families exactly.
- Apply one-sided binomial confidence bounds when Monte Carlo determines whether a requirement is met.
- Keep BCa and permutation results as sensitivities.

**Limitation**

I did not execute every r2 family because their grids and retention rules do not yet exist. The witness uses exact two-point families required by r2.

### Attack G — transport and pair replacement

**Method**

I combined an explicit mock request state machine with current official OpenRouter documentation.

**State-machine outputs**

| Event | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Proven pre-dispatch failure | Retry same arm from clean snapshot and same lineage |
| Provider accepted / no response body | Pair state unknown; invalidate whole pair |
| Malformed valid response | Score arm failure; do not reroll |
| Endpoint mismatch | Invalidate pair |
| Response/prompt cache hit | Invalidate pair |
| Transform or middle-out compression | Invalidate pair |
| Provider fallback | Invalidate pair |

Official documentation confirms the relevant hazards:

- Provider fallbacks are enabled by default unless disabled, and `require_parameters` is not the default ([provider routing](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection)).
- Router metadata can expose requested routing, endpoints, attempts, and pipeline stages, but has documented enablement and response-shape conditions ([router metadata](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/router-metadata)).
- Response caching has explicit cache statuses and can return a new generation ID with zero usage ([response caching](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/response-caching)).
- Prompt caching can be automatic and provider-dependent ([prompt caching](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/best-practices/prompt-caching)).
- Middle-out transformation/compression must be explicitly controlled ([message transforms](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/message-transforms)).
- Disconnects and timeouts can occur after dispatch, while fallback can occur before tokens are returned ([errors and debugging](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api_reference/errors-and-debugging)).

**Selection-bias execution**

Construct 100 scheduled target pairs:

- 95 neutral pairs with `delta=0`.
- 5 hard TN pairs with `delta=-1`.
- Only those 5 produce accepted/no-body ambiguity.
- Invalid rate is exactly 5%, so r2 does not stop because it stops only when the rate is greater than 5%.
- Replace them with five neutral reserve pairs.

Output:

- Target-set mean: `-5/100 = -0.05`.
- Reported replacement-set mean: `0`.
- Bias: `+0.05`, favoring TN.

Reversing the five hard-pair deltas to `+1` produces bias `-0.05`, favoring F.

**Interpretation**

Whole-pair invalidation protects within-pair symmetry but does not protect the target estimand when invalidity is related to arm behavior, order, payload pressure, latency, or outcome difficulty.

**Smallest repair**

- Preserve the original frozen target cohort as the estimand.
- Use reserves only to maintain nominal precision.
- Report invalidity by arm, order, payload utilization, pressure stratum, and failure stage.
- Compute a worst-case interval assigning every missing target delta anywhere in `[-1,1]`.
- Refuse win or kill unless the decision survives that interval.
- Stop immediately on evidence that invalidity is outcome-, arm-, or order-correlated, even below 5%.

**Limitation**

I did not send live provider requests. The state-machine assumptions were checked against official documentation, while the bias result is mathematical and does not depend on a live provider.

## 8. WOULD YOU DESIGN IT DIFFERENTLY?

Yes.

The primary contrast should be:

`flat append-only public history + deterministic replay + bounded flat traversal`

versus:

`typed-native immutable evidence + typed relationships and native closure`.

That design asks whether typed structure adds value beyond the strongest cheap representation-neutral continuity mechanism. It is both truer and cheaper than making TN earn wins from correction restoration and dependency closure that a small flat journal and bounded BFS already provide.

Current-only F remains useful only as a mechanism ablation: it measures the value of retaining public history at all, not the value of typed-native storage.

## 9. Omission attack

- **Strong simple comparator omitted:** `F+`, an append-only flat primitive-event journal with deterministic replay, a current flat projection, and bounded ID-adjacency traversal. StateFuse’s matched resolver/raw-log methodology is direct supporting precedent ([StateFuse](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05844)).
- **Hidden confound favoring F:** TN’s verbose type, edge, provenance, and currentness encoding can consume the equal token budget without adding useful exposed semantics.
- **Hidden confound favoring TN:** The generator’s update ontology mirrors TN while F is prohibited flat replay and ordinary traversal.
- **Persuasive false-positive story:** Retraction cases and two-hop prerequisites make current-only F return `unknown` or omit closure; TN exceeds `+0.03`, but F+ would restore/reach the same facts and tie.
- **Way to unfairly kill a useful typed kernel:** TN hits `budget_failure` because of an inefficient native serialization or its hardest pressure cells are disproportionately invalidated and replaced.
- **Footgun not caught by the gates:** Arm/order/pressure-correlated whole-pair replacement can bias the mean by `±0.05` while remaining at the allowed 5% invalidity rate.
- **One no-paid test without which I refuse pilot inference:** Inject accepted/no-body ambiguity as a deterministic function of arm, order, payload pressure, and latent delta; require the final decision to survive worst-case assignment of every invalid target pair in `[-1,1]`.
- **Mechanism to borrow directly from prior art:** StateFuse-style matched raw-log/materialized-view baselines using the same resolver and verification path; PM-Bench’s prospective set-F1/ledger framing is also directly reusable ([StateFuse](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05844), [PM-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12385)).
- **Machinery to delete:** The separate paid chronological-history diagnostic after F+ becomes primary. Retain current-only F as an unpaid ablation.

## 11. Residual risks and explicit non-claims

Even after the repairs, a positive result would establish only that the complete TN package improves prospective action selection for:

- The frozen model and provider endpoint.
- The frozen deterministic admission/reducer logic.
- The declared synthetic and hand-authored workload mixture.
- The selected packet budget and output cap.
- The tested transition grammar and score.
- The tested process-cold reconstruction contract.

It would not establish:

- That graph/typed memory is generally superior.
- That typed representation alone caused the gain; native closure, currentness, provenance, and encoding remain a package.
- Cross-model, cross-provider, multilingual, or production generality.
- Benefits for learned writers, learned retrievers, compaction, conversational memory, or natural-language admission.
- Production durability, security, privacy, latency, or lifecycle fitness.
- That the selected schema is Pareto-optimal in information density.

A negative result would mean that TN did not earn its additional complexity within this bounded envelope. It would not establish that typed continuity is useless for deeper graphs, larger budgets, higher-conflict worlds, auditing, explanation, or learned systems.

TF remains descriptive unless its subset allocation and precision rule are repaired. Synthetic-generator validity remains bounded even with hand-authored and multi-generator controls.
